01

The student problem

Students weren't lacking resources. They were navigating coursework, pacing, goals, and support across too many places without a clear next step.

Design question

How do you give learners enough structure to build momentum without creating one more thing to manage?

02

What I designed + how I led it

I created the original FLIGHT student experience and core Microsoft Planner framework, then helped lead its evolution from pilot into a broader cross-functional student-success initiative.

My role

Experience design · Planner framework · iteration · project coordination · training · rollout

Champion network

Mentor champions tested the experience, surfaced feedback, supported adoption, and connected design decisions to day-to-day student support.

Built with

Mentors · instructors · champions · SharePoint and automation partners · technical implementation · QA and rollout teams

The individual Planner connected with student SharePoint resources, a Faculty Hub, course-resource access, and human support. Technical implementation and content contributions were collaborative.

03

More wasn't better

Student and mentor feedback helped shape the D336 iteration: keep the learner phases and human support, but simplify overlapping tasks and guidance shown before it was needed.

Retained

Clear phases, next steps, and routes to human support.

Simplified

Overlapping tasks and premature guidance.

Why

Feedback highlighted access friction, checkbox timing, and assessment-readiness uncertainty.

The best iteration wasn't adding more. It was deciding what could come out.

04

What we observed

Early internal feedback noted students scheduling objective assessments ahead of their course-end goals, attending cohorts, using Planners regularly, and asking more strategic questions in mentor meetings. It also surfaced access delays, confusion about checkbox timing and assessment readiness, and a need for clearer micro-goals.

05

Scaling the experience + what I learned

Scaling FLIGHT required more than another Planner template. Champions brought field feedback into the design, while shared templates, faculty training, communication, reviews, and QA helped coordinate implementation. I worked across project phases, resources, workflows, and stakeholder updates; SharePoint development, automation, instructor content, and technical rollout remained collaborative.

Completion Pathways partnership: Our FLIGHT team has partnered with Completion Pathways to align student planning, course resources, and faculty support rather than develop two separate student-support experiences. Together, we are working toward a more connected approach that can scale across programs and courses.

I started by designing the tool. I ended up thinking about the system around the tool.
