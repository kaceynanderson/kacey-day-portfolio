01

The challenge

New programs create large amounts of information across requirements, outcomes, learner context, assessments, faculty expectations, stakeholders, and launch requirements. Faculty need more than a collection of program facts. They need usable readiness.

Current application

The readiness model is currently being applied to BSAIIO and BSAISEC mentor-readiness training. The earlier BSAIE experience serves as a predecessor and reference, not a course to copy wholesale.

03

My role

I lead the Training Pod's mentor-readiness work for new School of Technology program launches, translating program requirements and source material into structured faculty learning experiences and a repeatable development process.

For BSAIIO and BSAISEC, that includes separate Loop workspaces and Rise shells, section assignments, fact sheets, course blueprints, source gathering, unresolved-question tracking, QA, and coordination across two builds that need consistency without erasing program differences.

What I own

Learning architecture · program research · source validation · templates · training development · standards · QA · cross-functional coordination · implementation

Program content, publishing, review, and approval remain shared work with the appropriate partners.

05

Creating a source of truth

Don't design from assumptions.

The Program Fact Sheet comes first. Program information is researched, sourced, verified, and marked unknown when it cannot yet be confirmed. That keeps uncertain details from getting copied into multiple lessons and becoming harder to unwind later.

07

Designing for real learners

Academic readiness doesn't automatically equal available capacity.

Faculty preparation needs to account for the learner who can do the work and still has constraints around work, family, confidence, pacing, financial pressure, or time. Current readiness content explicitly separates academic capability from available capacity and evaluates acceleration through performance, consistency, engagement, workload, and time-management evidence—not enthusiasm alone.

08

Standardize vs. customize

What should become a system, and what still needs to stay specific to the program?

Standardize

  • Source verification
  • Learning architecture
  • Shared quality standards
  • QA and review practices
  • Reusable learning patterns
  • Path to publication

Customize

  • Program details and pathways
  • Assessments and credentials
  • Challenging courses and sequences
  • Learner considerations
  • Career outcomes
  • Program-specific scenarios

09

Designing the experience

Scenario pattern

Student question: “I had to do an Intro Term, am I behind?”

The learning pattern is not recall-only. Mentors practice how to normalize the concern, reframe what the pathway means, then move forward into purposeful term planning.

Why this matters

Knowledge checks are designed to mirror the actual work and connect directly to the lesson objective, so the experience is about making better mentor decisions—not memorizing program trivia.

A concrete QA catch

The reusable fact-sheet example deliberately flags a conflicting objective-assessment count—one lesson says 16 while another says 15—and instructs the builder to confirm the final number instead of carrying an inconsistency into the published training.

That is the point of the source-of-truth step: uncertainty is visible early enough to resolve.

The template itself says the BSAIE examples are there to show the pattern—not to copy.

Current evidence

The same readiness model is now being applied across the simultaneous BSAIIO and BSAISEC builds. I am not presenting launch outcomes until they are available and cleared.

14

What I learned

The part I became most interested in wasn't just building the training. It was figuring out which parts of the process shouldn't have to be reinvented next time.

Building BSAIIO and BSAISEC at the same time made the difference really clear. Some things should be repeatable: source verification, learning architecture, design standards, QA, and the path to publication. Other things have to stay specific to the program and the learners it serves.

That's when this stopped feeling like two courses and started feeling like a readiness system.
